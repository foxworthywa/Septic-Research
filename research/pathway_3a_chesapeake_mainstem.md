# Pathway 3a — Chesapeake Bay mainstem and ESVA Bayside tributaries

## Mechanism of harm

Failing onsite wastewater treatment systems (OWTS) on Virginia's Eastern Shore (ESVA) export nitrogen — and to a lesser but increasing extent phosphorus — to the Chesapeake Bay mainstem and its Bayside tributaries through two coupled delivery modes. First, the dominant mode on ESVA is diffuse submarine and sub-tidal groundwater discharge: partially treated effluent enters the shallow Columbia surficial aquifer as nitrified nitrate, travels along short (typically <2 km) and geochemically oxic flow paths, and is discharged directly through creek-bottom sediments and shoreline seeps into Pocomoke Sound, Tangier Sound, and the small tidal creeks that drain the western peninsula (Onancock, Pungoteague, Nassawadox, Cherrystone, Old Plantation) (Speiran 1996; Richardson 1994; Mitchell et al. 2021). Because riparian denitrification is spatially confined to thin carbon-rich strips, most shallow septic N bypasses the last-meter denitrification zone that the Chesapeake Bay Program (CBP) Phase 6 watershed model applies to "land-to-water" septic delivery (CBP 2017; Ator et al. 2020). Second, surface-water delivery occurs where failing systems back up into lawns, storm drains, or farm ditches that drain to the tidal creeks during heavy rainfall and flood-tide events, bypassing the subsurface treatment train altogether.

Once in tidal waters, this anthropogenic nitrogen drives the classical Chesapeake eutrophication cascade (Kemp et al. 2005; Boesch 2019). Elevated dissolved inorganic nitrogen (DIN) stimulates phytoplankton production in the euphotic surface layer; the resulting organic matter sinks, is remineralized by heterotrophic bacteria in the pycnocline and bottom water, and consumes dissolved oxygen faster than stratified summer circulation can resupply it, producing the seasonal "dead zone." Decades of monitoring have established a tight, quasi-linear relationship between January–May total nitrogen load to the Bay and summer hypoxic volume (Murphy et al. 2011; Frankel et al. 2022). A nontrivial share of the integrated N load is legacy groundwater nitrogen in transit — the groundwater reservoir attenuates with decadal lag — and septic systems, which release N directly to groundwater with no point-source controls, are a disproportionate contributor to that legacy stock relative to their nominal annual loading fraction (Sanford & Pope 2013; Ator et al. 2020).

The eutrophication cascade has several specific symptoms relevant to ESVA Bayside waters. (1) Chlorophyll-a and primary productivity rise with DIN, especially in the shallow, well-mixed tidal creeks where light is rarely limiting. (2) Harmful algal bloom (HAB) species — notably the mixotrophic dinoflagellates *Prorocentrum minimum* (mahogany tide) and *Karlodinium veneficum*, and in low-salinity reaches *Microcystis aeruginosa* — are promoted by elevated DIN/DIP ratios and by organic-N forms characteristic of wastewater (Glibert et al. 2001, 2014; Heil et al. 2005). *Karlodinium* blooms have caused documented fish kills in Bay tributaries and produce karlotoxins at ecologically relevant concentrations. (3) Shading from phytoplankton blooms and attached epiphytes, amplified by nutrient loading, drives the long-running decline of submerged aquatic vegetation (SAV) — eelgrass (*Zostera marina*) at higher salinities and widgeongrass (*Ruppia maritima*) in mesohaline reaches — with cascading consequences for juvenile blue crabs, bay scallops, and waterfowl (Orth et al. 2017; Lefcheck et al. 2018; Hensel et al. 2023). (4) Hypoxia exacerbates phosphorus release from bottom sediments, closing a positive feedback that delays recovery even after external loads decline (Testa et al. 2017). (5) Low DO and sulfide stress filter feeders on oyster reefs and degrade nursery habitat for blue crab and striped bass (summarized in Pathways 4 and 5).

Phosphorus contributions from ESVA septic systems to the Bay mainstem are quantitatively smaller than N but not negligible. Sandy, low-Fe/Al Columbia aquifer soils offer finite P-sorption capacity; aged drainfields can reach P breakthrough and deliver dissolved reactive P via groundwater (Robertson et al. 1998; Lusk et al. 2017). Because the Chesapeake's mesohaline reaches are seasonally P-limited in spring, even modest P additions from failing systems can shift bloom phenology and composition (Malone et al. 1996; Harding et al. 2016).
