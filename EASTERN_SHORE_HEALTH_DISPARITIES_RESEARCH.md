# Health Outcomes and Disparities on Virginia's Eastern Shore
## Accomack County and Northampton County vs. Virginia State Averages

**Research compiled: March 12, 2026**

## 1. COUNTY HEALTH RANKINGS OVERVIEW

Both Accomack and Northampton counties consistently rank among the **least healthy counties in Virginia** (bottom quartile of 133 ranked counties).

**2022 County Health Rankings (Robert Wood Johnson Foundation):**
- Accomack: Ranked **103rd** in health outcomes, **102nd** in health factors (out of 133)
- Northampton: Bottom quartile for both health outcomes and health factors
- Both counties are in the bottom 25% of Virginia for health factors

## 7. HEALTHCARE ACCESS

### Primary Care Physician Ratios

|---|---|---|---|
| Population-to-PCP ratio | **2,310:1** | 840:1 | 1,310:1 |
| Uninsured rate | **15%** | **13%** | 9% |

- Accomack County has **nearly twice** the state's population-to-physician ratio, indicating a severe primary care shortage.
- Uninsured rates are **44-67% higher** than the state average.

### Hospital Infrastructure

- **Riverside Shore Memorial Hospital** (Onancock, VA) is the **only hospital** on Virginia's Eastern Shore.
  - 52 private inpatient rooms (10 critical care, 6 mother/baby)
  - 13 private ER treatment rooms
  - ~500 employees
  - Serves both Accomack and Northampton counties (~45,000 residents)

### Eastern Shore Rural Health (ESRH) -- Safety Net Provider

- Operates **4 medical centers, 8 dental locations, 2 pharmacies, and 1 urgent care center**
- Provides sliding-fee discounts; no one turned away for inability to pay
- Federally Qualified Health Center (FQHC)

### Broader Context

- Virginia's Eastern Shore is designated as a **Health Professional Shortage Area (HPSA)**
- 44% of Virginia's census tracts lack adequate primary care access (VCU study, 2025)
- Rural census tracts have approximately **725 fewer patients capacity** than suburban tracts
- The AAMC projects a shortfall of up to **48,000 primary care physicians** nationally by 2034

Source: [Eastern Shore Rural Health](https://esrh.org/) | [Riverside Shore Memorial Hospital](https://www.riversideonline.com/en/locations/hospitals/riverside-shore-memorial-hospital) | [VCU Primary Care Study (2025)](https://news.vcu.edu/article/2025/07/primary-care-shortage-affects-44-of-virginias-neighbhorhoods-new-vcu-study-finds)

## 9. WATER QUALITY, SEPTIC SYSTEMS, AND HEALTH -- THE CONNECTION

### The Eastern Shore's Unique Vulnerability

- The Eastern Shore sits atop a **sole-source aquifer** (EPA-designated 1997) -- there are no significant freshwater streams as alternative supply.
- The shallow **Columbia aquifer** is vulnerable to contamination from septic systems, agricultural fertilizers, and pesticides.
- Groundwater provides at least **15 million gallons/day** withdrawn for public supply.
- Private well use in rural Virginia counties reaches **up to 80%** of the population.

### Septic System Failures

- The Accomack-Northampton Planning District Commission estimated approximately **200 homes** rely on privies or portable toilets, but "the full extent of the problem has not been documented."
- **Hotspots of annual septic failure** exist in areas with high groundwater tables and low suitability for septic systems.
- Historical practice: Companies pumping out septic tanks in Accomack County used to dump waste into **unlined lagoons**, allowing leachate from untreated human waste to seep into groundwater.
- Chincoteague pipes water **5 miles** from NASA Wallops Island wells because its own groundwater has been polluted by septic systems and brackish water intrusion.
- Rising sea levels and increasing precipitation are expected to **increase septic failure rates** in coastal Virginia.

### Nitrate Contamination Data

- **Nitrate-nitrogen** is the primary contaminant of concern in the Columbia aquifer.
- Sources: septic systems, agricultural fertilizers, manure storage, animal disposal, septage lagoons, and landfills.
- In Virginia, **~6% of tested private wells** (1,128 out of ~19,000) have nitrate levels above 5 mg/L -- a level studies have found potentially dangerous.
- The USGS found that nearly **one-fifth of shallow drinking water wells** in agricultural areas likely exceed the legal limit for nitrate (10 mg/L).
- Virginia requires only a **one-time test for coliform bacteria** for new wells -- no nitrate testing required, and no ongoing testing mandate.

### Nitrate-Cancer Connection (Key Studies)

1. **Schullehner et al. (2018), International Journal of Cancer** -- Danish nationwide cohort study of 2.7 million adults:
   - Highest nitrate exposure group had **HR 1.16** (95% CI: 1.08-1.25) for colorectal cancer
   - Statistically significant increased risks found at levels **above 3.87 mg/L** -- well below the 50 mg/L drinking water standard
   - [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29435982/)

2. **EWG Analysis (peer-reviewed)**:
   - Nitrate in US drinking water may cause up to **12,594 cancers per year** at a cost of $1.5 billion
   - **Colorectal cancer accounts for four-fifths** of nitrate-attributable cancer cases
   - [EWG Research](https://www.ewg.org/research/nitrate-us-tap-water-may-cause-more-12500-cancers-year)

3. **2022 Meta-analysis (ScienceDirect)**:
   - Colorectal cancer risk increased by **2.4% per unit increase** in nitrate concentration
   - [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1877782122000534)

4. **Wisconsin Rural Women Study (McElroy et al.)**:
   - Increased risk of **proximal colon cancer** among women with nitrate levels >10 mg/L
   - [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19108561/)

### EPA Regulatory Gap

- The EPA's nitrate MCL of **10 mg/L** was set in 1992 based on a **1962 recommendation** to prevent methemoglobinemia (blue baby syndrome) -- not cancer.
- The standard has **never been updated** despite decades of new epidemiological evidence linking lower levels to cancer risk.
- Research suggests health risks at levels as low as **3.87-5 mg/L**.

Source: [Center for Progressive Reform -- Private Wells in Virginia](https://progressivereform.org/publications/private-wells-va-data-gaps-public-health-concerns/) | [USGS Virginia Eastern Shore Groundwater](https://www.usgs.gov/centers/virginia-and-west-virginia-water-science-center/science/virginia-eastern-shore-groundwater) | [Virginia Wastewater Infrastructure Working Group Report (2021)](https://rga.lis.virginia.gov/Published/2021/RD883/PDF)

## 10. ENVIRONMENTAL JUSTICE AND EQUITY

### Disproportionate Burden

- Low-income and minority communities face **disproportionately high pollutant exposures** in drinking water.
- Communities with higher proportions of Hispanic residents tend to be served by water systems with **higher nitrate levels**.
- Private well owners bear the **full financial burden** of testing and remediation -- a significant equity concern for low-income households.
- There are "hundreds of examples of high-nitrate wells located in tracts with **high poverty and unemployment** and where access to health care is difficult."

### The Compounding Effect on the Eastern Shore

The Eastern Shore's health disparities do not exist in isolation. They compound:

1. **Low income** (median HH income 37-39% below state average) limits ability to test/treat well water
2. **High poverty** (up to 17.1%) means residents cannot afford alternative water sources
3. **Limited healthcare access** (1 hospital, severe PCP shortage) means diseases from water contamination may go undiagnosed
4. **High chronic disease burden** (obesity 40%, diabetes, heart disease elevated) creates a population already vulnerable to additional health insults
5. **Aging population** (25-29% over 65) is more susceptible to health effects of contaminated water
6. **No regulatory requirement** for private well testing means contamination goes undetected
7. **Racial disparities** (Black residents lose 4-5 years of life expectancy vs. White residents) overlap with environmental exposure patterns

Source: [Environmental Health Journal -- Socioeconomic Disparities in Nitrate Levels](https://ehjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12940-018-0442-6)
