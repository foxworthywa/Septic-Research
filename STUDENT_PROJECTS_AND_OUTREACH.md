# ESCC Student Project Ideas & Outreach Activities

Brainstormed project ideas for Eastern Shore Community College undergraduate students and outreach/education activities aligned with the EI Climate Collaborative ESVA Septic Project (2026-2028).

**Guiding principle:** Prioritize projects that **directly and materially improve** septic system health and water quality on the Shore -- not just study the problem. Research projects are valuable, but every project should have a clear path from data to action.

---

## Direct-Action Projects (Highest Priority)

These projects produce immediate, tangible benefit for Shore residents. They should be the first ones staffed and funded.

| # | Project | What Students Would Do | Why It's High-Impact |
|---|---------|----------------------|---------------------|
| 1 | **Free Well Water Testing Program** | Collect and test well water samples at community events (Earth Day, SepticSmart Month, Septic Suppers) and through door-to-door visits. Provide residents with their nitrate, coliform, and basic water quality results. Connect those with concerning results to next steps (filtration, pump-out, VDH follow-up). | **Makes the invisible visible.** Most Shore residents have never tested their water (Virginia doesn't require it). 43% of tested wells already show contamination. A test result is the "blood test moment" that motivates action -- the same way a child's lead exposure test motivates lead paint remediation. This is the single most powerful behavior-change tool available to the project. |
| 2 | **Door-to-Door Pump-Out Enrollment** | Visit homes in the StoryMap's highest-risk zones. Explain the septic-well connection using plain language and the "same aquifer" message. Help residents sign up on the spot for A-NPDC's free/discounted pump-out program. Track enrollment rates by neighborhood. | **Directly prevents septic failures.** VDH outreach letters achieved only 25% response. In-person visits are dramatically more effective. Every pump-out extends system life, reduces nitrogen load by 5%, and costs the homeowner $0 through existing programs. A $400 pump-out prevents a $20K-$40K replacement. |
| 3 | **Subsidy Navigator / Application Assistance** | Get trained on available funding programs (Virginia Indemnification Fund, USDA Rural Development, A-NPDC DEQ grants). Help residents complete applications at kitchen tables, community events, and ESRH clinic waiting rooms. Track applications submitted and funded. | **Connects existing money to people who need it.** Programs exist but are underused. Research shows willingness to replace a failing system jumps from 36% to 72% when a $5,000 subsidy is offered. The barrier isn't money -- it's navigating the paperwork. |
| 4 | **Ditch Survey & Maintenance Work Days** | Survey and map drainage ditch conditions in priority neighborhoods (GPS, photos, condition rating). Identify worst blockages. Organize community volunteer days to clear and re-grade ditches. Measure before/after standing water with simple sensors or photo documentation. | **Directly reduces flooding pressure on septic systems.** Targeted ditch maintenance reduces rainfall-driven flooding by up to 10%. Physical, visible work that builds community relationships and produces measurable results. |

## Outreach & Education Activities

### The Core Message

All outreach should center on this: **"Your septic system and your well use the same water. A $400 pump-out protects your family's health."**

Supporting messages:
- 43% of tested Shore wells are already contaminated. Most wells have never been tested.
- Nitrate from failing septics is linked to colorectal cancer at levels below the EPA limit.
- A $400 pump-out every 3-5 years prevents a $20,000-$40,000 system replacement.
- Free pump-out and repair assistance programs exist. We can help you sign up today.
- The Shore's aquifer is the only water source. There is no backup.

## Notes

- All outreach should emphasize the **"educate and notify, not enforce"** approach agreed upon with VDH
- Frame as **family health protection**, not environmental regulation or property value threat
- Materials should be accessible and avoid creating anxiety about enforcement (per VDH's "do no harm" concerns)
- Spanish-language versions of key materials should be developed where feasible
- Student co-authorship on articles and presentations builds their portfolios and deepens community trust
- **Track outcomes:** Number of wells tested, pump-outs enrolled, assistance applications submitted, ditches cleared. These are the project's most meaningful metrics of impact.
