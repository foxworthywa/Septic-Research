## Overview

This repository contains the complete document set for a **UVA Environmental Institute Climate Collaborative** grant project titled **"Co-Producing Equitable Solutions to Septic System Failures: Towards a Regional Resilience Hub."** The project is led by **Dr. Majid Shafiee-Jood** (UVA) and focuses on climate-driven septic system failures on **Virginia's Eastern Shore (ESVA)**. It is funded at **$500,000 over 2.5 years (2026-2028)**.

The project builds on a prior NSF Coastlines and People Climate Equity (CoPe) project that identified failing septic systems as a top community concern. Over 18,000 households in Accomack and Northampton Counties depend on septic systems, and aging infrastructure combined with rising groundwater, sea level rise, and intensified flooding is causing widespread failures -- contaminating wells, polluting the Chesapeake Bay, and deepening infrastructure inequities in underserved communities.

## Folder 3: Proposal (Full Grant Submission)

### EI-ClimateCollaborative_ESVASeptic.pdf / Narrative_Final.docx
The **complete grant proposal** (PDF is the formatted version; .docx is the editable source). Key elements:

**Four-stage vision:**
1. Diagnostic Stage (completed via prior NSF project)
2. **Action Stage (this proposal)** -- translates diagnostics into tools and demonstrations
3. Expansion Stage (future)
4. Regionalization Stage (long-term Mid-Atlantic Coastal Wastewater Resilience Hub)

**Four research thrusts:**
- **Thrust 1:** Deploy prototype sensors (groundwater, soil moisture, septic tank levels) at 6-10 households; build an AI-enabled early warning system
- **Thrust 2:** Extend groundwater modeling to map failure hotspots under sea level rise; co-develop an interactive decision-support platform
- **Thrust 3:** Digitize septic/well records into a unified geospatial database; identify data gaps
- **Thrust 4:** Map financing pathways, identify equity gaps, seed regional coordination

**Full research team:** Shafiee-Jood (PI), Wilson, Culver, Alemazkoor (AI/ML), Mitchell (VIMS), Reilly (UMD)

**Community engagement team:** Wilson, Cora Baird, Elizabeth Richardson, Alex Foxworthy

### Letter_ANPDC.pdf
**Support letter from the Accomack-Northampton Planning District Commission** (Elaine K.N. Meil, Executive Director). Notes the urgency of failing septic systems in the region and A-NPDC's prior work on septic upgrades for low-to-moderate income households through a VA DEQ grant.

### Letter_ESCC.pdf

## Key Themes Across All Documents

1. **Climate-driven infrastructure failure:** Rising groundwater, sea level rise, and increased flooding are causing septic systems to fail across Virginia's Eastern Shore
2. **Environmental justice:** Underserved rural communities bear disproportionate risk and cost
3. **Community co-production:** Deep emphasis on partnering with residents, not just studying them
4. **Technology + engagement:** Combining AI/sensor monitoring with education and community organizing
5. **Phased approach:** Current project is the "action stage" bridging prior diagnostics to future regional scaling
6. **Active collaboration:** VDH, ESCC, A-NPDC, Green Stream Technologies, and UVA/UMD/VIMS researchers are all engaged
